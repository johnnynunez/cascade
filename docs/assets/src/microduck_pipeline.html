Inside the MicroDuck agentic demo

What happens between one sentence and one learned step, for the request “Cross the backstage to the green marker without touching the equipment.”
1From words to a tool call

1Operator

“Cross the backstage to the green marker without touching the equipment.”
one request per duck; several ducks can share an explicit fleet board
➜

2LLM agent

Cascade's AgentOrchestrator: one LLM agent per duck, each with its own episodic memory and trace.

  • reads the request, the last tool receipts and a short list of robot tools
  • answers with a tool call, never with joint targets
➜

3Tool call

follow_route( waypoints=[[1.2, 0.4], [2.6, 0.2]])
All the LLM decides: which skill, which arguments. Other tools: observe, scan, my_map, plan_route, walk, turn, get_up…
➜

4Skill runtime

Cascade's per-robot MicroDuck runtime. Every tool:

  • runs a learned policy, never a scripted gait
  • returns a receipt with a fresh ToF frame, the pose estimate and what the duck saw
  • keeps a lockstep clock: physics advances only while a motion tool runs
2Sense with its own sensors only nothing from the simulator reaches the agent

5Onboard sensors

  • head RGB camera, 640×360 at 15 Hz (82° wide-angle lens), rendered by OVRTX from the live Newton poses
  • ToF 8×8 (VL53L8CX model, 45°×45°, up to 4 m) at 15 Hz
  • IMU in the trunk: gyro + accelerometer, with noise and residual bias
  • joint encoders: kinematics place the camera and ToF on the body
➜

6Where am I? cuVSLAM

Its own pose estimate, in its start frame:

  • heading: gyro integrated on SO(3), tilt corrected by the accelerometer (Mahony)
  • position: leg odometry from the encoders
  • NVIDIA cuVSLAM RGBD odometry on the head image, depth from its own ToF zones, gated against the gyro and the legs
  • tracking loss is reported, never fixed from simulator truth
measured error: 0.24–0.55 m over 5–15 m
➜

7What is around me?

YOLOE-11s, an open-vocabulary detector, runs prompt-free on the head image.

  • range from its own ToF zone, or where the box bottom meets the floor at the estimated pose
  • detections go into the duck's own memory
➜

8Own map, own plan

A log-odds grid built only from its own ToF frames.

  • cells: floor, obstacle (above floor + 4 cm), free, drop-off
  • A* on that map proposes waypoints, obstacles inflated 0.14 m
  • the agent decides whether to follow them
3Turn a command into steps the part most people never see

9Velocity command

follow_route steers toward each waypoint on the estimated pose: a small heading/speed loop, disclosed in every receipt.

cmd = (vx, vy, ωz)
  • walk / turn: the agent picks the twist and duration itself
➜

10Learned walking

An RL policy trained in Isaac Lab, used unchanged:

  • 61 inputs: IMU, joint angles and speeds, previous actions, the command
  • 14 outputs: joint targets for 10 leg and 4 neck/head joints
  • 50 Hz; at zero twist it keeps balancing
  • also learned: get up, onboard-sensor walking, skating
➜
Cascade guards

11Safety guards

  • ToF guard: forward travel stops with an obstacle under 0.22 m ahead or a drop-off under ~0.45 m
  • map guard: stops when its own map shows equipment within 0.13 m and closing
  • turning in place stays allowed
  • after a fall: the get-up policy
➜

12Servo model

Each of the 14 servos follows the pinned BAM actuator model (Rhoban fit of the MicroDuck servo).

target q* → motor torque τ
  • friction and supply voltage included, so the sim legs behave like the real servos
➜

13Newton physics

Isaac Lab 3.0 on NVIDIA Newton (MuJoCo Warp solver):

  • 5 ms physics step; the policy runs every 4 steps
  • real sole–floor contacts: the duck can trip and fall
  • one Newton world per walking duck
Feedback loop: the sensors are rendered again from the new poses at 15 Hz, the estimate and the map update, then the next command follows.
4Where the walking comes from offline, before the demo

14Train in simulation

PPO (rsl_rl) in Isaac Lab 3.0 on Newton, thousands of ducks in parallel, with the same BAM servo model in the loop.

  • flat and rough floors, ramps, steps; the canonical MicroDuck.usd from Pollen Robotics
  • separate policies for walking, getting up, the onboard-sensor walker and skating
➜

15Export and pin

Each policy is exported to ONNX and pinned by SHA-256.

  • the demo loads it unchanged: no gait or pose is scripted or repaired
  • a changed checkpoint is a new identity and needs a new evaluation
5Check, then answer

16Verify on the recorded physics

Did the duck really reach the marker without touching anything? Simulator truth is used here only, after the run:

pose within 0.35 m of the markeruprightclearance to every obstacle box
The agent's own claim (task_done) is shown next to the verdict; a claim the check refutes is reported as a failure.
➜

17Replay to video

Recorded at 50 Hz (16 body poses per duck), replayed through OVRTX (RTX) into a 1080p review video.

  • panels show the exact policy inputs (ToF 8×8, head RGB) and label what is illustrative
➜

18Agent answers

“I reached the green marker.”
If the check disagrees, the video shows FAILED next to the claim.
Honest agentic robotics: the agent sees only the duck's own sensors and its own pose estimate, every joint target comes from a learned policy, and every result is checked against the physics.
