## Artifact and construction

`assets/grasp_geometry/rebot_rs_fingers_physx.json` preserves every nominal component and adds a certified convex hull of every returned PhysX component: 40 components per finger: eight nominal, sixteen from NV09 on x86_64, and sixteen from Spark03 on aarch64. It does not replace the physical colliders or the nominal mesh. It contains no new contact margin and leaves the 0.1 mm opening feedback envelope unchanged.

The schema-2 compact input `physx_finger_cooking_source.json` retains all returned vertices, indices and polygon planes, exact prim identities, the mesh-to-body and audited zero-stroke mesh-to-TCP transforms, USD attributes, SDK source hashes, request settings and receipt hashes. Every derived component identifies its export and original hull index. Each export independently binds units, engine, robot, producer epoch, per-finger mesh/transform/parameter metadata, and raw hull counts/hashes. No convex is deduplicated across exports or replaced by a convex hull of the whole union. The raw SDK polygon planes do not enclose all their own vertices (residuals reach about 0.314 mm). They are provenance only. The builder recomputes QHull planes, verifies every vertex and uses the existing `1e-12 m` floating-point construction allowance. It never drops or joggles a degenerate component. Convexity then covers all faces and interiors of the returned convexes.

Sources checked by the runtime include the nominal JSON, compact cooking input, URDF/STL and seven tracked USD layers. No SDK import or cooking request is introduced into grasp planning. The runtime selects the new artifact only for the validated PhysX clock; the nominal path remains available for other consumers and does not claim coverage of PhysX cooking.

Attempt evidence records hashes of both geometry artifacts and the compact cooking input. Portable runtime bundles require and distribute all three JSON files; their URDF/STL and USD dependencies remain in the existing pinned asset inventory.

Build and check without Kit or a live bridge:

```sh
python scripts/build_physx_finger_geometry.py
python scripts/build_physx_finger_geometry.py --check
```

`--check` validates kinematic metadata, source/provenance fields, preservation of all nominal components, unit normals, tight supports and coverage of every derived vertex. It avoids comparing the order of reconstructed QHull planes across platforms.

## Required passive admission

Before new physical acceptance on Spark, acquire a separately guarded representation from that producer and audit it:

```sh
python scripts/build_physx_finger_geometry.py \
  --audit-representation /path/to/passive/representation.json \
  --output /path/to/new/coverage.json
```

This command has no network or Kit access. It requires unchanged metric source geometry and physics attributes, matching robot identity/epoch/engine, matching transforms and successful representation results. Every entire local convex must fit in **one** artifact component. Allowing different vertices to fall in different components of a union would not certify the intervening faces/interior, so that case is rejected. The only numerical allowance is the existing construction allowance. A failed check requires investigation and a reviewed artifact update; the tool never expands the envelope to make a check pass.

Both retained NV09 and Spark03 exports pass containment against the current artifact. This is passive admission of these two requested representations, not a guarantee for every future cooking result. Local admission also does not establish a direct active-shape mapping, unseen-surface clearance or contact-force behavior. The separately measured PhysX contact offsets were 1.362500 mm per shape, with rest offsets zero; their sum is not added to this artifact and is not treated as proof of the force that compressed the finger.

## ARM admission and the retained negative result

A separate passive Spark process used source `477c88fe40092fdaad99c0f777a6f1c3b9a41224`, a verified isolated checkout, and the original PhysX/camera settings. It requested three derived representations in 0.257592 s, obtained six RGBD frames, and closed its own process normally. Code, scene assets, producer identity, geometry, original memory/proof/trace and Chrome identity passed their guards. It sent no actuator commands. Both fingers again returned sixteen hulls, and the can one. All single-body tensor offsets matched NV09: 1.362500 mm contact offset and zero rest offset.

The initial 24-component artifact **failed** passive ARM coverage: only 6/16 left and 14/16 right convexes fit wholly in a single component. That result remains unchanged. Fifteen ARM left vertices and one right vertex were outside every old component; the largest minimum halfspace residuals were 0.686077 mm and 0.311910 mm. These residuals are not Euclidean distances. Some other failures merely lack whole-convex containment in one component; vertex membership in a union cannot certify the connecting faces/interior.

The reviewed correction retains both complete derived exports, producing 40 components per finger without changing the construction allowance. Audits of both original exports pass. The earlier setup failure (LFS pointer stat metadata) and the first passive metadata failure (an absent ARM `.pyi` stub) remain retained separately. The successful ARM capture hashes the actual loaded binary binding, Python module and extension configuration; no geometric check was made optional.
